A Connecticut Story · 1810–1880

From Windsor to the World

A Connecticut schoolmaster whose life changed China and Japan.

Samuel Robbins Brown (1810–1880) — born in East Windsor, Connecticut; principal of China’s first Protestant school; the man who brought the first Chinese students to America; translator of the Japanese New Testament.

I · Roots

The Man from Windsor

Born in East Windsor, Connecticut, on June 16, 1810, Samuel Robbins Brown graduated from Yale College in 1832 and belonged to the first graduating class of Union Theological Seminary. Before sailing for China he taught for four years at the New York Institution for the Deaf and Dumb (1834–1838) — a schoolmaster by training, and a schoolmaster he would remain all his life.

II · China

Principal of the Morrison School

In 1838 Brown sailed for Guangzhou under the Morrison Education Society and opened the first Protestant school in the Chinese Empire — at Macao in 1839, moved to Hong Kong in 1842. Its annual reports, published in the Chinese Repository from 1840 to 1846, record one of the earliest experiments in modern education on Chinese soil.

III · The Crossing

Three Boys Who Crossed the Ocean

In 1847 his wife’s failing health forced the family home. Brown did not return alone: three of his pupils — Yung Wing (容闳), Wong Shing (黄胜), and Wong Foon (黄宽) — crossed the Pacific with him. He enrolled them at Monson Academy in Massachusetts.

In 1854 Yung Wing graduated from Yale College — the first Chinese student to graduate from an American university. Those three boys were the beginning of the Chinese educational mission to America.

IV · Upstate New York

A College for Women

Brown labored for the founding of a college for women — first at Auburn, then at Elmira, New York. That school is Elmira College today.

V · Japan

Twenty Years in Japan

After the Harris Treaty of 1858 opened Japan, Brown sailed for Yokohama under the Dutch Reformed mission. For twenty years (1859–1879) he ran a school whose hundreds of pupils became leaders of modern Japan, translated the New Testament into Japanese, and helped found the Asiatic Society of Japan. Several of his English pupils were among the first Japanese Christians baptized, at Yokohama in 1872.
